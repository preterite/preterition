I've used Anthropic's Claude to help me put together a visualization of my recent linking and citing practices. In the accompanying diagram, each column keeps its own time scale. The diagram offers no comment on the arguments themselves: those are in the center column, to be read beyond the diagram. [Pop out the full figure](/weblog/assets/citation-figure-full.svg){: .citation-figure-open} for individual post citations.

{% include citation-figure.html %}

*A note on method:* the interactive diagram took me less than an hour to create with Claude. I already had the clean data in my research vault; Claude wrote the JavaScript and CSS and scripted the extraction from my BibTeX sources database through the blog posts to the Mermaid-generated SVG. The minimal human labor cost of adaptation came from prior choices I've made in tooling and organizing and indexing the vault. I'd suggest that with properly tended data, one might easily apply the knowledge-delivery practices proposed by Ridolfo and DeVoss to technologies of rhetorical and economic self-appropriation.
