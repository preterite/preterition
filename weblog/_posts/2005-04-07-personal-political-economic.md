Geoffrey Nunberg just did a [fine piece](http://www-csli.stanford.edu/~nunberg/personal.html) on NPR about the discourse of the "personal" versus the "private" and the accompanying rhetorical concerns of ownership. His piece stands as good evidence why [copy](http://www.thecopyfight.com/wordpress/)[fighters](http://www.corante.com/copyfight/) -- of whom, yes, I'll [finally admit](/weblog/archives/2004/06/05/more-on-the-party-line/) to being one -- need to be thinking about the work of both [Peter Elbow](/weblog/archives/2005/03/31/personal-writing-theory-and-method/) and [J. K. Gibson-Graham](http://www.communityeconomies.org/index.html), about how the act of engagement with ongoing discussions about intellectual property constitutes the seam or suture joining personal, political, and economic concerns.
