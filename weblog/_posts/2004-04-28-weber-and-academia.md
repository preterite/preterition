Via [Amanda](http://householdopera.typepad.com/about.html) at [Household Opera](http://householdopera.typepad.com/household_opera/) (congratulations on [the fellowship](http://householdopera.typepad.com/household_opera/2004/04/an_announcement.html), Amanda!) comes [a link](http://householdopera.typepad.com/household_opera/2004/04/post_of_utter_m.html) to the [interesting perspective on &#8220;Academic Calvinism&#8221;](http://www.crookedtimber.org/archives/001766.html) offered at [Crooked Timber](http://www.crookedtimber.org/), discussing the same [Chronicle story](http://chronicle.com/temp/email.php?id=xsvzl412963d3sb8hhgwrus0stnwe0op) on the [Invisible Adjunct](http://www.invisibleadjunct.com/) that&#8217;s been making the rounds in [other places](http://www.culturecat.net/node/view/374):

&#8220;Some tenured or tenure track commenters on IA’s site were quite convinced that the distinction between adjuncts and tenure track faculty reflected the judgement of the market on their respective quality as academics. This is Max Weber’s thesis on the origins of capitalism replayed as farce. Weber argued that Calvinist theology provided capitalism’s tutelary spirit. Calvinist beliefs in predestination led believers to distinguish between the elect and the preterite - those who were destined to go to heaven, and those who were destined to go to hell. Because it was impossible to be sure whether they were going to ascend to paradise or to burn, Calvinists sought evidence that they were favoured by God through accumulating goods without consuming them. If you did well in worldly affairs, you could take this as a sign of God’s favour.&#8221;
