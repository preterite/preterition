For perhaps [obvious reasons](https://zenodo.org/records/23663062), I've been experimenting with building what [recent](/weblog/2026/10/31/hey-pretty-agent-memory-part-3.html) [posts](/weblog/2026/11/07/arc-bends-toward-agent-memory-part-4.html) have characterized as research instruments. The instruments, and the readings they produce, are longer-term goals. In the shorter term, I've been drafting methodological approaches to those research instruments as what I'd call toys.

One of the toys, hidden in plain sight beside this post's thicket of ~~bad~~ dad puns, does what I think are interesting things with the [concept](/weblog/2022/11/29/ultimate-preterition.html) that governs this site and weblog's name. I'll accordingly refrain, and instead borrow [a famous bridge from Marx](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch01.htm#S1). 

> *Let us take two different strings of text, and assume that their relation, whatever it may be, can be represented by an equation in which a given measure of the first text is equated with some measure of the second. The equation would signify that a common element of identical magnitude* (Größe) *exists in aspects of the two texts, and that both are therefore equal to a third, which in itself is neither the one nor the other, so that each must therefore be reducible to the third.*[^1]

While the method isn't entirely firm, I'm digging the vibe. The bridge remixes my recent riffs on incommensurability and claims versus value: when one spins two texts beside each other, what can one read or measure as the masked intervening differend?

The *tl;dr* repeats a refrain from the avant-garde tradition: toys may become tools; systematic play may attune one to unexpected insights. 

&nbsp; 

[^1]: Distortions are mine alone. When I cite Marx on the weblog, I'll almost always rely on the wording from the paper [Ben Fowkes translation](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/261069/capital-by-karl-marx-translated-by-ben-fowkes-introduction-by-ernest-mandel/) I prefer; Moore-Aveling is [the one available online](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/), so it's the one to which I link. I haven't yet finished the [recent Reitter translation](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691190075/capital) from Princeton, but so far it's impressive.
