The most ambitious and talented American fiction writer of recent memory has [committed](http://www.latimes.com/news/obituaries/la-me-wallace14-2008sep14,0,246155.story) [suicide](http://www.marginalia.org/dfw_kenyon_commencement.html). People of my generation said he was our [Pynchon](http://www.hyperarts.com/thomas-pynchon/) or [Gaddis](http://www.williamgaddis.org/), and to a degree, I agreed: while I found his essays and short fiction simultaneously amazing and infuriating, *[Infinite Jest](http://www.thehowlingfantods.com/inf.htm)* was the book that got me excited again about the possibilities of American fiction; the book that once again -- after far too long -- made false Hofstadter's claim that in a time when the ocean of knowledge has burst its shores, the encyclopedic novel is no longer possible; that we could never again have another *Don Quixote*, *Middlemarch*, *Bleak House*, *Zauberberg*, *Gravity's Rainbow*, or any other work that adequately encompassed the world and knowledge and spirit of its age. *Infinite Jest* did so, brilliantly.

I'm sad to know he's gone.
