### {% raw %}{{ content }}{% endraw %}

My fellow UMass alum [Lisa Dush](https://doi.org/10.58680/ccc201527641) opens her 2015 essay on content with a WordPress writer who has come to understand good writing as posts optimized for reach through categories, tags, and search metadata (Dush 2015, 173). I resembled Dush's writer for two decades. This place ran on WordPress until this summer, when I converted the posts to static pages, dropping the tags and consolidating the categories. Dush's definition of content encompasses the professions that now work with writing, and she notes [Tim Kreider](https://www.nytimes.com/2013/10/27/opinion/sunday/slaves-of-the-internet-unite.html)'s complaint that the term assigned him and his artist friends the role of "content providers" on the assumption that delivery matters and the writing is "filler, stuff to stick between banner ads" (Kreider, quoted in Dush 2015, 174).

Content, in Dush's definition, has four characteristics: conditional, computable, networked, and commodified. She hedges the last, noting content "is---or will be---commodified" (178). I think she's correct in [her future-tense hedge](/weblog/2026/10/04/appropriation-position-and-relation.html). Commodification is an event at a position rather than a property of a text: a text becomes a commodity when someone holding the position to sell it does so, and until then it stays in Dush's future tense. She traces an example through [John Trimbur](https://doi.org/10.58680/ccc20001415)'s distinction between use value and exchange value: in networked space a video or a tweet is judged on the number of clicks and retweets it accumulates, its exchange value rendered as brand reach or ad revenue (178). The endgame Dush fears for writers follows, that writing "becomes something one does for free" (191). Counting [the stream of clicks](/weblog/series/in-the-clickstream.html) sets a tweet, an essay, and an advertisement as equals; the writer of each unpaid. The machine audiences Dush names rank and match (176). A ranking is a count, and whoever holds the ranking does the valuing, since [no machine can take up a use](/weblog/2026/10/03/valuation-labor-and-measure.html).

A ledger reaches only what it can commensurate. Thamus judged an art he couldn't practice. Content is writing made commensurable. A ledger that counts content has already set unlike labor equal, at a ratio a person chose. *"This is worth more than that; this **will be** worth more than that."*

### Another five-man has come

Here are the laws I've so far stated in the recent economics posts, numbered by clause so subsequent passages can reference without quoting:

1. *[Valuation: Labor and Measure](/weblog/2026/10/03/valuation-labor-and-measure.html)*. (1.1) Taking up a use is a valuation, and valuations vary with uses. (1.2) Value is the labor time a society must spend, which a machine can count and no machine can take up. (1.3) The line between what is counted and what is judged falls where a society stops being willing to spend, drawn by a party with a stake.
2. *[Appropriation: Position and Relation](/weblog/2026/10/04/appropriation-position-and-relation.html)*. (2.1) A party with a stake is a position in a relation, which persons occupy and leave. (2.2) The surplus made at any stage of the cycle is appropriated at that stage by whoever holds the position to do so, the maker included, and by more than one party, since a text is not used up in the taking. (2.3) The share a maker takes in the making, where it is realized, no ledger can reach.

Each law begins where the one before it ended. The party with a stake who draws the line in 1.3 becomes the position in 2.1, and the ledger that can't reach the maker's share in 2.3 opens this post's law. Both pick up from [the law stated](/weblog/2026/09/29/transformation-storage-and-time.html) for [the technology series](/weblog/series/toward-a-theory-of-technology.html), that stored labor becomes capital only in a relation, at a use, and the time it stores is owned by whoever takes up the use.

### Three errors, corrected

I figure it's apparent I've been trying to do some systematizing. A theory of economy needs a theory of technology; a theory of technology needs [a theory of the subject](/weblog/series/toward-a-theory-of-the-subject.html). In my recent re-reading and writing-through that systematizing, I've encountered some previous mistaken assertions I made.

I closed my 2014 chapter on digital literacy instruction in Afghanistan with the claim that "the value produced from those three inputs changes form via three fundamental economic problems," which I named **substitution**, **transformation**, and **aggregation** ([Edwards 2014](https://doi.org/10.4018/978-1-4666-4916-3.ch011), 217). Two years later, in an article on digital reading, I named them again, with aggregation glossed as a question of canonicity, substitution as the problem "we recognize in the Borges character Pierre Menard, who composes, without copying, a portion of the *Don Quixote*" and in anxieties about plagiarism, and transformation as "how the labor of reading becomes value as manifested in capital" ([Edwards 2016](https://doi.org/10.1215/15314200-3158701), 147). The chapter's version of substitution said that "labor can change its value depending upon who performs it, and depending upon who appropriates that value, and at what point in the cycle of production, distribution, use, and re-production" (Edwards 2014, 217). The cycle was right. I had the names wrong. (Whitman, multitudes, etc.)

The substitution problem conflated reduction with appropriation. How labor changes value with who performs it is what economists call the *reduction problem*: unlike kinds of labor equalized, so that a quantity of one counts as some multiple of the other (sounds familiar, no?). Who appropriates the value, and at what point in the cycle, was the [*appropriation question*](/weblog/2026/10/04/appropriation-position-and-relation.html). Neither is the economists' *substitution*, capital taking the place of labor; the chapter used that sense in passing, where [computers substitute capital-intensive processes for labor-intensive ones](/weblog/2026/09/28/substitution-capital-and-labor.html). The transformation problem, the Afghan instructors' hours becoming the academy's curriculum, was [living labor becoming stored labor](/weblog/2026/09/29/transformation-storage-and-time.html); the chapter never reached the economists' *transformation problem*, values into prices of production. The aggregation problem bundled the Cambridge capital controversy, the fallacy of composition that reasons from the individual to the group, and the production boundary for [Duncan Ironmonger](https://doi.org/10.1080/13545709610001707756)'s uncounted household labor. Each is a **commensuration failure**: an item set equal to an unlike other.

### Woodward, Washington, Latifah as economic tradition

[Discretization cuts a continuous thing into units, and quantification measures the units](/weblog/2026/09/27/composition-noun-and-verb.html). Commensuration is the operation that follows. [Wendy Espeland and Mitchell Stevens](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.soc.24.1.313) define commensuration as the expression or measurement of characteristics normally represented by different units according to a common metric, and they distinguish it from quantification by whether the items linked by the numbers seem already alike. Where the items seem unlike, we notice the commensuration; where they seem alike, "we imagine we are simply counting or measuring something" (Espeland and Stevens 1998, 315--17). A census looks like quantifying people. A word count looks like quantifying words. A grade looks like quantifying an essay.

Money looks like the equalizer. The credit hour seems to contradict my placement of the equalizing step at exchange, where money moves against the text: the credit hour sets a unit of a learner's time equal to a unit of credential, and no money moves when the registrar enters it. A grade sets an essay equal to a letter, and nothing changes hands either. [Marx](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch03.htm) resolves the contradiction: "It is not money that renders the commodities commensurable. Quite the contrary. Because all commodities, as values, are objectified human labour, and therefore in themselves commensurable, their values can be communally measured in one and the same specific commodity, and this commodity can be converted into the common measure of their values, that is into money" (Marx 1993, 188).

Money is the market's instance, the price that stands in for the whole, and a ratio can be set without it. [Kelly Ritter](https://www.jstor.org/stable/30037888) discovered such an instance in 2005 on SwapTermpapers.com, a site that let a student trade an essay of her own for an essay from its database and advertised that old papers were "lying around without any value. We put value on your work!" (SwapTermpapers, quoted in Ritter 2005, 621). A one-to-one ratio, no cash transacted.

Who sets the ratio? Content strategy, as Dush reports, works from a figure its practitioners call "the quad," substance and structure on one side and workflow and governance on the other, with a core strategy at the center (Dush 2015, 186, after Halvorson and Rach). The quad cuts the writing process into stages different from composition's. Composition discretized the process for pedagogy, so a teacher could intervene at drafting or at revision. The quad discretizes it for allocation, so each quadrant can be assigned to a position, and the position that governs may not be a writer's. Dush quotes Rahel Anne Bailie and Noz Urbina on the allocation: "Writers can't be experts at everything related to content" (Bailie and Urbina, quoted in Dush 2015, 184), and the expertise writers can't be expected to hold goes to a content strategist. A bar from a role brings a middleman, and the middleman holds the governance quadrant, where the writing's value is adjudicated: the ratio, unnamed and hidden from the writer by a discretized position.

### Multiplied simple labor

Marx's version of the operation says nobody sets the ratio. In the [first chapter of *Capital*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch01.htm#S2), skilled labor counts as simple labor "intensified, or rather multiplied" (Marx 1993, 135). As for the multipliers, the proportions in which different kinds of labor reduce to simple labor are "established by a social process that goes on behind the backs of the producers," and they "appear to the producers to have been handed down by tradition" (135). The Moore and Aveling translation at that link has the proportions "fixed by custom," and loses Ben Fowkes's "to the producers" from the Penguin edition, Marx's *ihnen*. The pronoun matters. Marx inscribes an access clause on the producers: what they can see of the ratio is a tradition, and the social process that set it is closed to them. My account requires an agent, because its laws place a party with a stake at every line drawn (1.3) and a position at every appropriation (2.2). The ratio gets set by whoever holds the ledger: the teacher at the grade, the registrar at the credit hour, the platform at the click. The writers can neither see the ratio set nor contest it, and seeing and contesting come apart: a grade is published to the student and rarely contested, and the ranking behind a feed can't be seen.

Laws 1.2 and 1.3 rest on Marx's two senses of socially necessary labor time. The weaver's sense, in *Capital* volume 1, is the time required under the normal conditions of production. The other comes from volume 3, where Marx notes of ground rent that "necessary labour-time takes on a different meaning here," since "society can spend only so much of its total labour-time on one particular kind of product" ([Marx 1992](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1894-c3/ch37.htm), 774). A machine can count the weaver's time. Society draws the line on spending, and a party with a stake stands in place of Marx's social need. Marx quotes [Aristotle](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.5.v.html) doubting that things so unlike could be set equal at all (Marx 1993, 151). The labors the reduction joins are unlike, and nothing in them says what the multiple should be.

### Instruments of darkness

A university carries two strictly parallel ledgers on a student. The credit hour prices the path: so many hours in a seat across so many weeks, denominated the way a wage is, [a unit of the learner's time set against a unit of the institution's](/weblog/2026/09/22/certification-by-plan.html). The grade proxies the product: a letter attributed to an essay, standing in for whatever the writer did to make it. Neither instrument reads the other, and neither reaches the share a writer takes in the making.

Grain and exchange keep the share out of reach. The grain is contingent: the credit hour counts in hours and the grade in letters, and between the discretized units the writer's labor goes unrecorded, a protection that shrinks as the learning management system, the process-tracking assessment, and the proctoring camera arrive to [record what the hour used to leave alone](/weblog/2026/09/20/forgone-optimization.html). [The exchange line](/weblog/2026/10/04/appropriation-position-and-relation.html) [Margaret Reid](https://archive.org/details/economicsofhouse00reid) drew and Ironmonger carried into the national accounts is structural: a third person can't be hired to learn on your behalf, because the benefit accrues to whoever does the learning. Nobody can be paid to take up the use, so the use is never set equal to anything. The share is safe for the same reason it's unpaid.

Peter Elbow tried to stop giving the grade. Under his contract grading, the crux of the course "is no longer that commodity I've always hated" ([Elbow 1993](https://www.jstor.org/stable/378503), 196), a number ranking the quality of a student's writing on one scale. Elbow had already explained the ranking's unreliability: holistic scorers agree because they've been trained to "stop reading the way they normally read" (188--89). Espeland and Stevens record the same qualm among faculty who object to ranking graduate students against benchmarks, a category they hold apart from comparison (Espeland and Stevens 1998, 328). An institution that can't read the product certifies the path, and ranks the product anyway.

In 2001 [Jeffrey Young](https://web.archive.org/web/20090305231710/http://chronicle.com:80/weekly/v47/i43/43a02601.htm) reported in the *Chronicle of Higher Education* that the University of Southern California had become a patron of Turnitin.com, joining faculty who said that "checking papers for cheating may soon become a routine part of grading" (Young, quoted in Ritter 2005, 618). Detection and grading converge.

### One essay, two ledgers

In November 2004 Elena Martinez told ABC's *20/20* that for three and a half years she had completed the papers and assignments for her former roommate, Elizabeth Paige Laurie, a granddaughter of Wal-Mart's co-founder Bud Walton, for about $20,000. Martinez had dropped out of USC because she couldn't afford the tuition. Laurie received a bachelor's degree from USC's Annenberg School in May 2004; nearly a year after the broadcast the university announced that she had voluntarily surrendered the degree ([AP via NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna9757284); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2004/12/04/4203407/usc-cheating-probe-focuses-on-wal-mart-heir)). Martinez said she had learned a great deal doing Laurie's work. The labor was priced and paid. The credential was attributed to the payer, then reclaimed. The laborer couldn't buy the credential her labor earned, and kept the share no ledger reached. [I wrote about it](/weblog/2005/01/05/the-athletes-labor.html).

In 1988 USC barred the student newspaper's research-assistance ads from using the words *writing*, *editing*, *research*, *assistance*, or *proofreading*. By 2001 it was buying the count (Ritter 2005, 617--18). Ban the words, buy the detector: both responses treat the text as the thing to police. The mill's ledger and the classroom's collide. Ritter, studying the online paper mills in 2005, saw the collision before the detectors did, though her essay equated economy to market. The mill's ledger carries the labor and no author: it pays its writers and sells their work to students seeking "already-finished, available-for-purchase papers by nameless and faceless authors" (Ritter 2005, 602). The classroom's ledger carries the author and no labor: it names a person as the origin of the text and never prices the work. Plagiarism is where those ledgers disagree over one essay, the product identical and the labor different, and the classroom is where the disagreement gets adjudicated by a grade. The paper mill and the ghostwriter are the same substitution of one writer's labor for another's, priced rather than policed.

The classroom's ledger carries the author because composition inherited an Author defined by the finished text. [Bruce Horner](https://ir.library.louisville.edu/faculty/62) links the concept of the autonomous Author to the removal of writing from the social material world, which redefined writing "from writing as an activity engaged in to writing as an object produced" (Horner 1997, 508--9), and Horner proposes investigating writing with students as social and material practice (526). A reader encounters an Author so defined in the object and nowhere else. The hours that made the object don't show in it, so a ledger that names the author has no column for them, and an object that arrived from somewhere else looks the same on the page. Adjudicating plagiarism then means inferring the hours from the text. [Menard's *Quixote*](https://archive.org/details/labyrinthsselect0000jorg) is the limit of that inference: the artifact matches Cervantes's word for word, and the labor behind it is another person's entire effort, none of which the page shows (Borges 1962, 36). The text ledger reads one entry. The labor ledger reads two.

Reid's third-person test sorts the essay from the learning. The essay is delegable, as the paper mill proves by pricing it; the learning is not, as Martinez's remark confirms from the delegate's position. The credential takes the delegable artifact as evidence of the non-delegable activity, and plagiarism policy polices that boundary from the artifact's end: it infers the labor from the text and then infers the intent from the labor, a judgment about an interior drawn from a product. [Raymond Williams](https://archive.org/details/problemsinmateri0000raym), in the sentence Horner quotes, provides the alternative: "the most important thing a worker ever produces is himself, himself in the fact of that kind of labour" (Williams, quoted in Horner 1997, 525). The mill can't deliver the share. Ritter's own students held a labor criterion for authorship without being taught one. In her survey, about a third of students counted co-writing as authorship and almost none counted pasted sources under one's own name (610), and a tenth said their papers in no way represented their identities as writers (611). Writing not deemed valuable invites the purchased paper, she concluded. When the assignment leaves nothing non-delegable, the protected share is already evacuated.

Plagiarism detection reads the text against the record of other texts and returns a similarity score, a count presented as a valuation. The mill sells that count back to the student: AuthenticEssays.com promised refunds if "even a couple of sentences" of a purchased paper matched an outside source word for word (AuthenticEssays, quoted in Ritter 2005, 622--23), its product certified against the same overlap the classroom polices. AI detection reads the text against a model of text and infers the producer, a probability presented as a judgment, authorship treated as a property of the product. In every case the holder reads the labor from the product.

That circumstance provides the warrant for this post's law: *(3.1) a ledger reaches only what it can commensurate; (3.2) whoever holds it sets the unlike labor of different writers equal at a ratio the writers can neither see set nor contest; and (3.3) the holder attributes that ratio to the product, which stands in for the labor no copy carries.*

### Scarce credit

Credit applies excludability to attribution. The text stays non-rival, since a copy takes nothing from the original, and the credit stays rival, since the position of author can be held by one name at a time. In 2005 I [argued](/weblog/2005/03/09/cccc-presentation-work.html) that plagiarism policy imposes an artificial scarcity on student writing to anchor it as a commodity. The refinement, twenty years on: the policy makes the credit scarce, and the text was never scarce at all. The Paper Store sold papers with the original author's name at the top and a warning at the bottom that no portion may be reprinted without proper attribution to the Paper Store (Ritter 2005, 609): the mill that paid for the labor claimed the attribution, and the student who bought the paper claimed it again. Elizabeth, one of Ritter's students, told of a graduate student who bought a paper, corrected its mistakes, handed it in, and got a B for half the work, and she asked the question the law answers: "Should they then receive half of the credit?" (Elizabeth, quoted in Ritter 2005, 612). Half the labor earned a whole credential, because the credential attributes a ratio to the product and the product can't say who made it.

Marx's credit, [the worker advancing labor-power before the wage](/weblog/2026/10/04/appropriation-position-and-relation.html), differs from the sense of credit as attribution withheld or granted by whoever holds the ledger. One word covers both because both are denominated in time: the worker's time advanced, the writer's time attributed.

### A zoetrope of value

A ratio attributed to the product is a price. [Collin Brooke](https://cgbrooke.substack.com/p/no-ai-is-not-more-persuasive) wrote in August that making persuasion measurable requires controlling so many variables that the quality measured gets reduced to something unrepresentative, and he asked by what metric the influences on a decision could be compared at all: the commensuration question. The control Collin described is a snapshot: the context held still so the count can be taken. [I replied there](https://cgbrooke.substack.com/p/no-ai-is-not-more-persuasive/comment/348835288), with Marx, that the economics of rational choice is itself digitized, a series of synchronic slices that can't account for change over time. A grade is a snapshot of an essay. A price is a snapshot of a commodity. A price settles at a moment while labor runs on through time, and a ledger that attributes the ratio to the product owes an account of the interval.
