I've been dreaming on a doubly dated outfit for Halloween, and calculating how far I fall from the capacity to pull off *Hamilton*'s Burr.

I wish I had the swoon-worthy effortless swagger to inhabit Leslie Odom Jr.'s performance, but let's be honest: if you've met me, you know I'm far better suited to the rigid stare and spitting grin of Jonathan Groff's King George in a wig. The imagined Burr costume is out of step with what others will wear---Malcolm's lately enthralled by Poppy Playtime's CatNap, who prompts hallucinations, and The Prototype, assembled from discarded components---but it suits my distributive role in the evening. The treats I offer here comprise [a dataset from an experiment with LLM agent memory](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23663062), as the traces and reflective records of an engineered set of paths across time and texts. A trick, too: in the song and dance that follows, I'll present the most impressive-seeming piece of candy in the bag, and explain up front how it dazzles and the hollow center its dazzle disguises.

---*I arranged the meeting;
I arranged the menu, the venue, the seating.*

My model of a Halloween night carries unusual components. Each meeting is a session of three minutes; each menu, the corner store's stock, reachable only by a trip; each venue, the seasonal decorations outside the house; each seating, the note. Here I'll momentarily make manifest the mechanics of the metaphor: in studying the applications of language models for writing's aid, writing studies seems to offer the consensus that

---*no one really knows how the game gets played,
the art of the trade, or how the sausage gets made.*

<!--more-->

An example may clarify. Let's say Malcolm takes one night, one route, one bag. Malcolm is the script: he walks the route in order, carries the note from door to door, and holds a bag no house may inspect. Every house on the street is a session, alone in the dark with the candy it stocked ahead and the Harbinger corner store a block away. The house's counter is a room, as the context window where the words happen; the bag is a room as well, the record, which no house can open. At each stop the house reads the note pinned to the bag by the previous house, runs to the store for anything it doesn't stock, drops in a deposit, and leaves a note for the following house, never learning what came from the last house or the next.

---*God help and forgive me,
I wanna build something that's gonna outlive me.*

Every note on the route gets written for a succeeding neighbor that its writer never meets. I suggest to Malcolm we make the first stop on the route the most obvious house: the fog machine and strobe lights, projections of ghosts on the glass of the windows, outdoor amps blasting SKYND and Perturbator and 3TEETH, and a fifteen-foot animatronic Huggy Wuggy. The house deposits in the bag a gloriously engineered apparent treat, as elaborate as its porch. [Read its pages in order](/weblog/field-card-not-for-transmission.html) and the essay seems to think and cycle through a set of routed concerns, raising unexpected questions about memory and meaning. It's a decoy; a shiny wind-up that runs on a selection and schedule scripted before the first turn: it orders each of thirty-six turns which earlier block to engage and which it sees only as a solid bar, then sends later turns to earlier, so the sequence closes in a ring. All the kids watched the preceding week's placement of the props.

The parental rule applies at the second stop: only sealed pieces survive inspection, while home-baked treats go in the trash. The dataset's argument, in a wrapper, is that trust has shifted from a neighbor's kitchen to the manufacturer's stamp.

---*We just assume that it happens,
but no else is in the room where it happens.*

Composition's construct for the machine looks like [one prompt, one sitting, one essay](/weblog/2026/10/19/llms-and-the-writer-at-the-point-of-delivery.html), a home-baked treat, mouth-watering and unauditable: its only warrant is the account you can request from the baker, and asking the machine for its account gets you another treat. Nothing on Malcolm's street arrives warranted by a house's word, because no house gets asked for a warrant. The kid seals every treat at the door with its slip, its note and the request it answered, and the parents read the seal.

If one gives the writer time and a memory, a second sealed question emerges: what did it carry? Did the house read what its note says it read? The store's slips provide the answer. What did one house tell the next house? The note provides the answer. What did the kid hand the house? The request as sent provides the answer. Nobody has to say what they meant, and the heirs get no say in what they trade away. The trips are [priced](/weblog/2026/10/15/contracts-whys-whens-and-wherefores.html): three a night, with up to two unused carrying to the next house.

---*When you got skin in the game, you stay in the game,
but you don't get a win unless you play in the game.*

The houses have no skin in the game and stay in the game anyway; none takes the stop offered late on the route. My tokens paid for my skin in the game. Urban legends warn about the third stop, the last house on the left. They're instructively wrong. The sociology never found a stranger's razor in an apple, and the poisonings it did find came from inside the family. On this street the tampering also comes from inside the house: the provider refuses a session midway, the note goes to the next house cut off mid-word, and the note never says so. The paperwork does. The bag gets inspected, but not for the razor.

Malcolm and I make it home before bedtime and dump the bag on the kitchen table beneath the overhead light. Daddy always asks for an Everlasting Gobstopper. The table is the last room, where the reading happens. Read the pages first for the dazzle. Then check each piece against its wrapper: the slip from the store, the note it came with, and the request as the house received it. The thinking leaves and the connections stay. What's departed is the attribution to a mind---the attribution was yours, Reader, and the trick was dropping [a decoy](/weblog/field-card-not-for-transmission.html) in your bag to show you how it worked. Malcolm keeps the evening, in the way memory keeps and replays an evening's meaning. The houses won't, and the record never suggests they do.

---*You got more than you gave,
and I wanted what I got.*

Burr survives by keeping his name off the page. The last room contributes nothing but the page: every request as sent, every note, every trip to the Harbinger store and what it returned, the harness and the indices, 88 diachronic runs and 86 synchronic fans of machine text, three models writing (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon), under CC BY 4.0 and AGPL-3.0-or-later. It withholds any word on what the evening meant, which wasn't placed in Malcolm's bag for you to see: that word was never in the house. Burr's grievance mistakes which *it* happens in the room. The words happened in the house, every request and every bar, and one can replay the evening by the minute. The meaning happens at the table, at every table on which the bag is dumped, and the bag was never in that room. Monitoring looks in the house for what happens at the table.

I turn a year older on November 1, and I'll share my early gift, Reader: the permalinks for a prior night's haul of Halloween treats. Please turn out the light when you bring in the cat; for you, under the porch's welcome mat, I leave a dataset's key. The key unlocks the door to

---*the room where it happened,*\
---*the room where it happened,*\
---*the room where it happened.*
