I had a fine time in San Francisco, and I'll share [John's sentiment](http://faculty.deanza.fhda.edu/jocalo/2005/03/17) that CCCC is much more than the sessions. While I didn't get a chance to chat with [Steve Krause](http://www.stevendkrause.com/academic/blog/), and I missed [Jenny Edbauer's](http://www.cwrl.utexas.edu/~edbauer/blogs/jenny/archives/001214.html) presentation, I was glad to finally meet [Derek Mueller](http://www.earthwidemoth.com/mt/) (whose presentation, it doesn't hurt to say [again](/weblog/archives/2005/03/19/4cs-evaluating-academic-weblogs/), totally rocked), [Daisy Pignetti](http://writingblog.org/doctordaisy/) (who is absolutely charming), and [Joanna Howard](http://twoyearcomp.blogspot.com/) (whose [Montgomery College](http://www.montgomerycollege.edu/) t-shirt John was wearing at the Thursday night meet-up in South Beach; I spent a semester at MC as an undergrad). And it was good to see [Brad](http://www.ccsn.nevada.edu/english/bleck.htm), [Charlie](http://cyberdash.com/), [Clancy](http://www.culturecat.net/), [Collin](http://wrt-brooke.syr.edu/cgbvb/), and [Dennis](http://jerz.setonhill.edu/weblog/index.jsp) again, as well as many other colleagues and instructors past and present.

And now, [as Collin points out](http://wrt-brooke.syr.edu/cgbvb/archives/2005/03/ccccelestial.html), it's time to start thinking about next year. I'll share Collin's sentiment that the "trend [...] towards increasingly arbitrary and unclear categories" on the CCCC Call for Proposals  is problematic, and I'll add a question: do the proposal form's "area clusters" perhaps actually hinder our disciplinary conversations? I noticed that a lot of bloggers went to a lot of the technology-focused panels, which of course is to be expected (it's become axiomatic that the thing bloggers most like to blog about is blogging) -- but I didn't see any panels that had only one or two tech presenters; the tech panels were all tech, all the time (somebody, please, correct me if I'm wrong), which I think makes for a sort of echo chamber effect. It can also lead to attitudes like the one I (perhaps mistakenly) perceived in [Anne Jones's troubling "dark ages" comment](/weblog/archives/2005/03/19/4cs-evaluating-academic-weblogs/); attitudes that pedagogies associated with digital technologies are somehow *beyond* rather than a part of composition's body of knowledge. I wonder what might happen when composition reaches the disciplinary point that the *New York Times* reached on March 24, when it [eliminated the Circuits section](http://poynter.org/forum/view_post.asp?id=9017) because of the way technology concerns had begun "migrating into the mainstream."
