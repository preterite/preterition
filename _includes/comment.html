{%- comment -%} _includes/comment.html -- one comment, pingback or trackback, rendered from its file under _data/welcomments//. Called with `c` (the record) and `all` (the post's whole thread, for resolving a reply's parent). The same include renders a comment written in 2004 and one that arrives next week, because both are files of one shape. A webmention, filed under _data/webmentions//, takes the same shape with a different `type` and a few fields of its own (see _scripts/fetch_webmentions.py). What each part says, and why it is text before it is style: the kind a pingback or trackback opens with the words "Pingback from" or "Trackback from", and a webmention with words for what it is -- "Reply from", "Liked by", "Mentioned by" and the rest -- so what it is reaches a reader who sees no colour and a screen reader alike. The class on the article is for the stylesheet; the words carry the meaning. the author linked to their own site where they gave one, as written, never rewritten: the site is theirs. rel="ugc" names the link for what it is. An owner's comment carries no site link -- the site it would link to is this one -- and says "(author)" instead, which is the visible form of the owner mark; the class is decoration on top of it. the time links the comment's own anchor, which is what a reader quotes or replies to, and its datetime attribute is the file's own stamp with its offset, so the instant is exact whatever the build machine's clock says. the reply "in reply to" links the parent's anchor. The thread renders flat and in order, the convention of the years most of it was written in; the link is what threading becomes when nothing nests. the message is Markdown in the file and HTML here. A mention fetched from the webmention receiver carries text from someone else's page, so it is escaped rather than converted, and cut at sixty words. the original a fetched mention links the page that sent it, labelled "original": the author link may lead to the author's home page rather than to what they wrote, and the cut text's remainder is there. reply the Reply link points at the courier's own reply page, which is where a reader with scripting off ends up; with scripting on, main.js intercepts it and sets the reply field of the form beneath the thread instead, so a reply never leaves the page. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign c = include.c -%} {%- assign kind = c.type | default: "" -%} {%- assign classes = "comment" -%} {%- if c.author.role == "owner" %}{% assign classes = classes | append: " comment--owner" %}{% endif -%} {%- if kind != "" %}{% assign classes = classes | append: " comment--" | append: kind %}{% endif -%}
