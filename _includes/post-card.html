{%- comment -%} _includes/post-card.html -- one weblog entry as a card, on the index and on its own page alike. Called with `post` (the entry) and `mode`, which is "index" or "entry"; the entry page also passes `body`, the layout's rendered content. One include rather than two blocks of markup, so the card is one rule: a reader moving from the index to an entry should meet the same object, and two copies of it would drift apart. The two pages differ in three things, and the include carries the difference rather than the callers: the byline the entry page adds "(weblog post, 2003-) Mike Edwards" beneath the title, for a reader arriving on an old link four levels down with no context about whose site this is. The index does not: its header already says so, and ten cards each repeating a name is noise. the body the entry renders whole; the index renders the lead, which is the author's own division at the read-more marker where one exists and the whole post where none does. The lead is crafted or it is dropped, never derived. the foot the entry closes with its older and newer neighbours, in the weblog convention; the index has the list itself. The date links its month's archive and each category links its own, so an entry is an entry point into the archive around it. The month address is derived from the date and the category address from the name, by the same slug rule the archive pages are built with, so the two cannot disagree. Jekyll's `previous` is the older entry and `next` the newer: the collection sorts oldest first, so the names read against `site.posts`, which the index walks newest first. The labels below say older and newer, and rel="prev" and rel="next" follow the same reading. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign p = include.post -%}
