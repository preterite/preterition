### Insurgent Rhetorics and Historical Materialism

_#Rhetops_ (University of Pittsburgh Press, 2019)

American military doctrine treats rhetoric as information moving through a
continuous present: Field Manual 3-24's attempt to historicize
counterinsurgency got stripped of citations prior to publication. Insurgent
propaganda publications like _Inspire_ do the opposite, grounding claims to
legitimacy in histories of oppression, and Western media respond by casting
the region of southwest Asia as fundamentally pre-modern. In
material-historical terms, rhetorical labor aggregates into circulating
rhetorical capital, and the distinction between the open hand of persuasion
and the closed fist of violence collapses: special operations kill the people
who constitute networks of rhetorical delivery, and _Inspire_'s enacted
persuasion delivers violent effects.

### Unpacking the Universal Library: Digital Reading and the Recirculation of Economic Value

_Pedagogy_ 16.1 (2016)

Recent arguments against digital reading practices rely on contradictory
implicit claims: that digital technologies harm the way we read, and that
reading is a set of monolithic and unchanging practices. Both claims are
mistaken. Digital reading practices illuminate the diverse forms of value in
the labor and capital associated with textual work.

### Economies of Writing, Without the Economics

Some Implications of Composition's Economic Discourse as Represented in _JAC_
32.3-4. _Rhetoric Review_ 33.3 (2014)

Composition's increasing engagement with economic concerns has not been
matched by any comparable attention to economic scholarship. The result is an
attenuated disciplinary rhetoric that represents economy as either beyond
intervention or as a metaphor for non-economic phenomena. A more rigorous
grounding in economic scholarship offers composition studies a rhetoric that
opens possibilities for economic agency.
